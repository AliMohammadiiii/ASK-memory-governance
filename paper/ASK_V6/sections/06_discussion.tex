\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What the evidence supports.} The decision record and its R1 replay are verified on one log, whose 883 surplus rows relative to the extraction cache are accounted for (822 replayed rows of stopped runs and 61 repeated decisions on cached extractions). The hidden MGP-6 v4.3 result shows that one frozen configuration classifies the three actions on a controlled synthetic construction with an accuracy near 0.85, the largest share of the errors (38 of 82) being gold-\abstain{} items on which the controller did not abstain. The LongMemEval run shows that the grader marked 128 of 180 answers correct (mostly by a model judge), and the decision log supports the descriptive statements about decision counts. None of these results compares ASK with an alternative write policy under matched conditions.

\paragraph{Limitations of the specification.} The reference protocol was first committed after the LongMemEval run and the MGP-6 hidden evaluation, is not called by any evaluated system, has a baseline validator that accepted all 19 tampering probes and checked the configuration identity for format only, does not persist its events, and defines a temporal mode without an implementation. The corrected validator is a patch written by us after the evaluations with the help of an AI research assistant; it is regression-tested on probes written by the same party, it has not been run on any evaluated store, and its requirement that unrelated records stay unchanged is a design choice that we have not yet decided to adopt. The evaluated store was an in-memory database per question, and the action log is written after, and not atomically with, the mutation. The released runtime code also differs from the code that produced the LongMemEval run: the run manifest records hashes of 17 code and protocol files, all of which we recovered from repository history and object storage, and the evaluated orchestrator differs from the released one by 912 changed lines after normalizing a package rename. The diffs and a manifest of the 17 files are in the supplement, but the behavioral equivalence of the two has not been shown. Ten of the 17 evaluated files exist in the development repository only as unreferenced objects of a cruft pack that is at risk of removal under default git maintenance (not tested); we recovered them by hash and ship copies.

\paragraph{Limitations of MGP-6.} MGP-6 v4.3 is synthetic, template-guided and labelled by a deterministic rule, so agreement between annotators does not validate the construct. The labelling rule makes the action a function of which two pooled sentences the dialogue indexes, and the live controller receives those sentences directly. A high score therefore is consistent with the model, together with the repository's public-evidence guard, applying the decision criteria to the indexed evidence, and it does not measure the ability to find the relevant evidence in free dialogue. We did not show that the distractor sentences cause the errors: the controller on the hidden split never receives them, and a check that every resolvable development error corresponds to a sentence in the same pool that is indexed elsewhere holds by the construction of the pool, so it does not support that explanation. The 2 $\times$ 2 diagnostic contrast used five substitute configurations of three models and the development split on which the live prompt was selected. Its design is not a clean factorial (the prompt factor is a bundle, the system label line differs across cells, and cell E is mismatched), the substitute models were chosen after the first results had been inspected, one configuration has a single repeat, and the primary parsing rule was adopted after outputs had been observed. It found routing-associated and prompt-associated differences that differ among the five configurations and, for Gemini at high reasoning, differ in sign depending on how replies are parsed, so it cannot be transferred to the manuscript model. The substitute-model results do not bear on the hidden accuracy of 0.848, because they come from different models, prompts and parsing rules. The two annotators were independent of the author according to study metadata. The release records use the identifiers \texttt{ALI} and \texttt{MOHAMMAD}, and the study owner confirms that \texttt{ALI} is not the author. A third independent human was designated as adjudicator, but no action-label disagreement arose and the recorded label-adjudication count is zero. These identity and independence statements are study metadata rather than properties authenticated by the release artifacts. Finally, MGP-6 v4.3 materializes no memory state, so its \keep{} label describes an expected preservation and not an observed one.

\paragraph{Limitations of the LongMemEval run.} The slice was reused from tuning, the run was controller-only, according to the study owner the manual adjudication was performed by an independent human reviewer, although the artifacts record no reviewer identity or date and the case selection is undocumented, and three baseline verdicts carry manual overrides that the study owner attributes to an independent human reviewer, with no reviewer identity, date, tool or selection criteria recorded in the available artifacts, the answer provider was not recorded, a request seed of 0 was sent but provider determinism is not established, and, according to the run manifest (which records one control set) and the study owner, the execution was interrupted and resumed under the same frozen controls; per-invocation control hashes were not recorded. The manifest records three invocations and incomplete operational metadata for the final one. The judge is a language model, and seven of the nine manual corrections fell in one question type. The final answer score is not evidence about the correctness of the intermediate memory state: a correct answer can follow from a wrong memory and the reverse.

\paragraph{Claims we do not make.} We do not claim priority for write governance, abstention, supersession, lineage, event records, replay or layered evaluation (Section~\ref{sec:related}). We do not claim that the interface is storage independent or controller independent, that it is durable, that it supports temporal queries, that evaluated systems conform to the protocol, that confidence scores are calibrated, or that the write policy causes downstream accuracy. We do not claim a homogeneous comparison of five systems on LongMemEval-M. We also report no hidden-split result for any configuration other than the one frozen before scoring.

\paragraph{Use of AI assistance.} AI research assistance (Claude Science, Anthropic) was used broadly in the audit and manuscript-preparation workflow, including assistance with code and evaluation tooling, substitute-model experiments and reanalyses, parser and validator corrections, literature and comparator review, LongMemEval provenance reconciliation, claim checking, figure and table preparation, and drafting. The author reviewed the resulting outputs, reran the reported checks where indicated, and is responsible for the manuscript.

\paragraph{What would strengthen the contribution.} A five-system run under one configuration on a fresh slice, with raw results reported first and an adjudication ledger; the evaluated controller run through the reference adapter and validator with persisted events and a corrected validator; and a demonstration that the combination of features supports a capability that the closest neighbors lack, such as conformance checking of a third-party store.
