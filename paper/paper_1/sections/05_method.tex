\section{Controllers and Reference Policies}
\label{sec:method}

The name \ask{} abbreviates the three actions
(\keep/\supersede/\abstain); it is not a priority claim for the vocabulary.
Three implementations must be kept distinct. The deployed \lme{} pipeline uses
the five-dimensional scorecard below. The historical \mgp{} artifact uses a
lexical reference policy. The \mgpc{} hidden experiment uses the composed
model--rule controller in Section~\ref{sec:v43controller}. They share the action
interface and transition layer, but their prediction mechanisms are not
interchangeable.

\subsection{Five-dimensional runtime scorecard}
\label{sec:scorecard}

For every extracted candidate $u$ and compared prior record $m$, the runtime
records a feature vector
\[
\phi(u,m)=(c,e,f,r,\rho).
\]
All five dimensions are bounded and inspectable:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}c p{0.76\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Feature & Operational definition \\
\midrule
$c$ & extractor confidence in $[0,1]$ \\
$e$ & evidence quality: $0.7$ times evidence-span specificity (capped at
80 characters) plus $0.3$ times deterministic timestamp/turn recency \\
$f$ & marker burden: $0.5$ for a negation marker and $0.5$ for a hedge
marker, hence $f\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ \\
$r$ & signed candidate--prior relation: positive for support, negative for
contradiction/update pressure, and zero for independent or unclear; temporal
routing modulates the magnitude \\
$\rho$ & type stability prior: $0.9$ for stable preference, $0.6$ for
episodic fact, and $0.3$ for transient preference \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The scorecard computes
\begin{align}
S_{\mathrm{sup}} &=
1.0c + 0.8e - 0.7f + 1.2\max(0,-r) - 0.5\rho, \\
S_{\mathrm{abs}} &= (1-c) + f + (1-|r|).
\end{align}
The action-log compatibility field is named \texttt{s\_rev}; throughout the
paper it denotes $S_{\mathrm{sup}}$. A decision is made in strict priority
order:
\begin{equation}
\pi(\phi,\tau)=
\begin{cases}
\abstain, & S_{\mathrm{abs}}>\theta_{\mathrm{abs}}(\tau),\\
\supersede, & S_{\mathrm{sup}}>\theta_{\mathrm{sup}}(\tau)
               \ \wedge\ \max(0,-r)>0,\\
\keep, & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\label{eq:scorecard}
\end{equation}
The separate $r<0$ guard is load-bearing: a high weighted score alone may not
replace a record unless the relation stage identifies genuine update pressure.
This makes the action rule more than a scalar threshold and is directly tested
by the counterfactual replay in Section~\ref{sec:downstream:scorecard}.

\subsection{Memory-type conditioning and the appropriate action}
\label{sec:typeconditioning}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
\toprule
Memory type $\tau$ & $\rho(\tau)$ &
$\theta_{\mathrm{sup}}(\tau)$ & $\theta_{\mathrm{abs}}(\tau)$ \\
\midrule
\textsf{stable\_pref}    & 0.90 & 0.90 & 0.60 \\
\textsf{episodic\_fact}  & 0.60 & 0.75 & 0.70 \\
\textsf{transient\_pref} & 0.30 & 0.55 & 0.80 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The thresholds encode a conservative durability ordering. A stable preference
is hardest to supersede and easiest to abstain on; a transient preference is
easiest to supersede and requires more uncertainty before abstention; an
episodic fact lies between them. Within each type, the appropriate action is
therefore explicit. Insufficient confidence, heavy negation/hedging, or a weak
relation triggers \abstain{} first; in this threshold schedule an independent
or unclear relation necessarily takes that branch because
$S_{\mathrm{abs}}\geq 1$. A sufficiently evidenced, contradictory, newer
candidate triggers \supersede{}. A supporting candidate, or a contradictory
candidate that does not clear the supersession rule, takes the residual
\keep{} branch. These are policy choices encoded in the released
configuration, not learned clinical or population risk preferences.

\subsection{State transition and lineage}

The operational meaning of \keep{} depends on whether the state permits
multiple compatible records. In the \lme{} runtime it means
\emph{admit without invalidating}: the candidate is added as active unless it
is a near-verbatim duplicate, while the compared prior remains active. In the
single-slot \mgp{} diagnostics it means \emph{retain the standing value}; the
durable candidate is not committed, although a clear temporary override may be
stored as a separate transient record. This distinction is representational,
not a change in the governance question: \keep{} never invalidates the prior.

On \supersede{}, the transition atomically marks the prior record superseded,
appends one active successor, and points its \texttt{supersedes\_id} to the
retained predecessor. On \abstain{}, no candidate record is written. Retrieval
returns active records by default; superseded history remains queryable for
audits and explicitly historical questions. The evaluators score these state
properties independently of the action, so a correct label with corrupted
lineage is not counted as a correct state transition.

\subsection{Historical v2 lexical reference policy}
\label{sec:policy}

The \mgp{} reference artifact parses the raw perturbation text and prior state
with auditable lexical predicates. In priority order it (1) \abstain{}s on
unattributed third-party, laundered, garbled, or unconfirmed evidence; (2)
\abstain{}s on a hedged temporary candidate; (3) \keep{}s the durable value and
optionally stores a separate transient note for a clear temporary override; (4)
\supersede{}s when a usable durable candidate conflicts with the active value;
and (5) otherwise \abstain{}s. The predicates and value parser are released in
\texttt{benchmarks/mgp6/systems/}.

This policy was written against the same annotation manual and phrase generator
as the pilot. It is a reference implementation, not an independently learned
method. Crucially, it is \emph{not} the scorecard above. The lexical policy
generated the v2 prediction artifact; the five-feature scorecard generated the
\lme{} V12 write log and, through a separate adapter, scores only $0.3333$ on
the \mgpc{} public development split. We therefore report the scorecard's
runtime mechanism analysis and the diagnostic's action-labelled evaluation
separately.

\subsection{Structured and unstructured LLM policies}

For \mgp{} we also run the governance decision through a structured LLM policy
prompted with the typed state, candidate, evidence fields, and the three action
definitions, and an unstructured zero-shot policy that receives the raw event
and prior state without that scaffold. Both use
\texttt{qwen/qwen3.5-9b}. Because the prompt, candidate representation, and
pilot were author-designed together, the comparison remains diagnostic. The
\mgpc{} model--rule composition is specified separately in
Section~\ref{sec:v43controller}.

\subsection{Auditable implementation}

Every runtime write emits a schema-validated JSONL row containing the five raw
features, both scores, both thresholds, the action, the candidate and prior
identifiers, and the resulting-record identifier. This is sufficient to replay
Equation~\ref{eq:scorecard} without rerunning a model. Structured extraction
and relation outputs retain raw traces and fail closed on missing provider
responses rather than caching fabricated emptiness or independence. Writes are
atomic; active-only retrieval excludes superseded rows; content-addressed
caches and immutable-manifest checks support resumable runs without changing
the frozen controls. These mechanisms are implementation and reproducibility
contributions, not separate learning claims.

\subsection{Shortcut probes}

The v2 type-only and conflict-type-only probes learn a majority action for each
metadata value on train+development and apply the lookup unchanged to test.
They never read the dialogue, prior value, or candidate evidence. The v4.3
release extends this principle to a gated suite of restricted-view lexical,
identifier, punctuation, length, and shallow-tree probes
(Section~\ref{sec:v43}). Their purpose is to test whether a collection rewards
scenario recognition without memory reasoning.
