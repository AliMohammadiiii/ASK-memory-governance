\section{Discussion, Threats to Validity, and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:limitations}

\subsection{What the evidence supports}

The evidence supports the usefulness of separating write, state, and answer
failures and the necessity of testing diagnostic shortcuts before interpreting
policy scores. It also supports within-pilot mechanism observations, such as
lineage corruption being invisible to action accuracy. It does not support
population accuracy, deployment safety, reference-policy superiority,
non-inferiority, or downstream benefit.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

\paragraph{Internal and construct validity.}
The same project designed the taxonomy, manual, generator, gold labels, and
reference policy. Conflict type perfectly predicts test actions, so item holdout
and high annotation agreement cannot establish construct validity.
Family-cluster bootstrap corrects one form of dependence but cannot repair the
deterministic family--label mapping.

\paragraph{External validity.}
\mgp{} is small, synthetic, English-only, single-step, and single-slot. It lacks
independent generation, natural dialogue, multi-step trajectories, multiple
model families, multiple seeds, and native external-system comparisons. None of
its rates estimates a deployment population.

\paragraph{Annotation.}
There is one independent human re-annotation of the test split plus a four-model
audit panel. Agreement may be high because the manual and family-to-label mapping
are deterministic. The model panel is not human inter-annotator agreement, and
one human annotator cannot establish multi-annotator reliability or construct
validity.

\paragraph{Statistical conclusion.}
There are only 48 test template families, and cluster intervals are
correspondingly wide. The reference--LLM comparison is a null, not evidence of
equality. Confidence calibration and cost matrices were not validated on
independent data, so they expose trade-offs rather than certify risk. The
one-sided upper bound attached to zero action false commits prevents a sample zero from
being presented as a safety guarantee.

\subsection{Ethics and scope}

The pilot contains synthetic, low-risk scenarios with no real personal data. Our
target is epistemic write correctness. Memory poisoning, multi-principal access
control, privacy, deletion compliance, and security governance are separate
threat models that we do not test. We make no production-safety claim.

\subsection{Successor-dataset gate}

A successor release must be constructed after the evaluated policies and
thresholds are frozen; use an independent generator and hidden labels; include
\keep, \supersede, and \abstain{} within every conflict family; add adversarial
minimal pairs and 5/10/20-step trajectories; obtain blinded labels from at least
three humans; and include natural or independently paraphrased dialogue. The
test set must remain sealed until all development choices are fixed. These are
requirements for stronger claims, not future-work decorations.

\subsection{Reproducibility statement}

The dataset, schema, validator, reference policies, and per-item predictions are
released. The construct-validity script regenerates shortcut tables,
family/minimal-pair cluster intervals, calibration, risk--coverage, and cost
sensitivity from those artifacts. The incomplete six-shot run and mapped
external adapters remain explicitly non-primary. Reproduction commands are in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro}. Independent construction, multi-human annotation, and
trajectory data are release gates for a successor benchmark rather than boxes
silently marked complete here.
