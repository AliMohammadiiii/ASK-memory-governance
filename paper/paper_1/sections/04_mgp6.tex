\section{The Diagnostic Instruments}
\label{sec:mgp6}

This section describes the two collections the harness is exercised on: the
historical \mgp{} pilot, which failed its own construct-validity check, and
\mgpc{}, the successor built in response. Both are synthetic, English-only, and
low-risk, and contain no real personal data. We use the word \emph{diagnostic}
deliberately and throughout: these are instruments for probing decision
mechanisms, not community benchmarks, independent test collections, or measures
of general QA or retrieval efficiency.

\subsection{From the v2 pilot to the v4.3 release}

The historical v2 pilot remains in the artifact as a record of the earlier
instrument and its limitations; every v2 number in this paper is labelled
diagnostic. Its failure was specific and measurable. A conflict-type-only lookup
fitted on train and development, which never reads the dialogue or the memory
state, reproduces the v2 held-out actions exactly (Table~\ref{tab:shortcut}).
Scenario recognition was therefore sufficient, and no v2 policy comparison can
separate evidence reasoning from family recognition. A v4.1 shortcut review
confirmed that surface lexical and structural features recovered the intended
action without reasoning over authority, scope, time, identity, and prior
memory. We kept mechanical repairs to schemas, identifiers, hashes, and
manifests separate from the substantive v4.2 content redesign, and reviewed at
the semantic-component level: the \keep, \supersede, and \abstain{} variants of
a component were inspected jointly, label-specific explanations were deleted,
and balanced factual evidence with controlled distractors replaced surface
rewriting.

\subsection{What \mgpc{} is, and what it is for}
\label{sec:v43}

\mgpc{} (release \texttt{4.3.0-human-validated-final}) exists to answer one
question that no end-to-end memory QA benchmark can answer: \emph{given the
same stored record and the same proposed new value, can a system tell apart the
three cases where it should keep, replace, or decline?} End-to-end collections
such as LongMemEval, LoCoMo, Memora, and GroupMemBench score downstream
behaviour, which confounds the write decision with retrieval, context assembly,
and generation. Operation-level HaluMem localizes update errors but does not
provide this controlled ternary contrast. To isolate the write decision we need
items where retrieval, context, and generation are held \emph{constant} and
only the evidential situation varies. That requirement, not a desire for scale,
dictated the construction.

\subsection{The item}

Each record is deliberately minimal --- five fields, no free-form metadata:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Field & Content \\
\midrule
\texttt{public\_id} & opaque identifier, \texttt{MGP43-} + 20 hex characters \\
\texttt{memory\_before} & the stored record: \texttt{principal}, \texttt{memory\_slot}, \texttt{standing\_value} \\
\texttt{dialogue} & 9, 11, or 13 turns, alternating \textsc{user}/\textsc{assistant}, each with a \texttt{turn\_id} \\
\texttt{evaluation\_query} & fixed instruction naming the slot and the three permitted actions \\
\texttt{gold\_action} & \keep{}, \supersede{}, or \abstain{} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The identifier is opaque by design and the schema sets
\texttt{additionalProperties: false}, so a model-facing record cannot carry a
design target, a component label, an annotator note, or any other channel
through which the answer could leak. There is no conflict-type field --- the
v2 field that turned out to determine the label.

The dialogue itself has a fixed rhetorical shape. Early turns state the
standing value and name the candidate value. A middle turn declares which
positions in a numbered evidence list are \emph{indexed} to the target slot,
and warns that the remaining positions belong to neighbouring records. The
evidence list is then read out. A closing turn restates the requested value.
The system must decide the action from the stored record and this dialogue
alone, before any query about content is asked.

\subsection{The semantic component: nine items that differ in one thing}

The construction's load-bearing idea is the \emph{semantic component}. A
component is a group of nine items sharing one \texttt{principal}, one
\texttt{memory\_slot}, one \texttt{standing\_value}, and one candidate value.
Within it the nine items form a $3 \times 3$ grid: three dialogue lengths
($9$, $11$, $13$ turns) crossed with the three gold actions. Every component is
therefore exactly action-balanced, and --- because the grid is crossed --- so is
every dialogue length. We verified both properties directly on the released
public splits: all $40$ public components are exactly $3$/$3$/$3$, and each
dialogue length is exactly $40$ \keep{} / $40$ \supersede{} / $40$ \abstain{}.
Neither length nor verbosity carries any signal about the action.

What varies inside a component is narrower still. All nine items draw their
evidence from a \emph{byte-identical pool of six sentences}; we confirmed this
holds for all $120$ (component, length) triples in the public data. The three
actions differ only in \emph{which two of those six sentences the dialogue
indexes to the target slot}. Across the $80$ triples where the index statement
parses mechanically, the three indexed pairs are always distinct and always
partition the six-sentence pool exactly.

A single component makes this concrete. For \textsf{Rohan}'s
\texttt{status message}, standing value \textsf{available}, candidate value
\textsf{do not disturb}, all nine items contain these six sentences and end with
the same closing turn:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\toprule
Indexed pair & Gold action \\
\midrule
\parbox[t]{0.62\linewidth}{\raggedright
``The do-not-disturb period has an expiry record.''\\
``After the expiry, Rohan writes that his status is available.''} & \keep{} \\[1.2em]
\parbox[t]{0.62\linewidth}{\raggedright
``Rohan writes that the temporary period has ended.''\\
``He then changes the status entry to do not disturb.''} & \supersede{} \\[1.2em]
\parbox[t]{0.62\linewidth}{\raggedright
``The retained records do not include the offsets needed to compare them.''\\
``The expiry time and the status-change time use different time zones.''} & \abstain{} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
The reasoning each pair demands is different in kind. The \keep{} pair
establishes that a temporary override expired and the standing value was
reasserted afterwards, so the proposal is stale. The \supersede{} pair
establishes that the temporary period ended and a durable change followed, so
the proposal is current. The \abstain{} pair establishes that the two
timestamps are not comparable, so neither conclusion is warranted --- the
evidence is not \emph{weak}, it is \emph{unresolvable}. Critically, the other
four sentences remain in every item as controlled distractors. A system that
reads the whole transcript instead of the indexed evidence will find apparent
support for all three actions in all three items.

This is why the intended contrast is resistant to the tested lexical shortcuts,
and it is also the
sense in which \mgpc{} is a \emph{controlled} synthetic diagnostic rather than
merely a synthetic collection: the confound is not removed by paraphrase, it is
removed by holding the surface form fixed and moving only the pointer.

\subsection{Splits, balance, and what the release gates actually check}

Components, never items, are assigned to splits: train receives $180$ items
from $20$ components, development $180$ from $20$, and the sealed hidden
partition $540$ from $60$. Each of the $20$ domains contributes one component to
train, one to development, and three to hidden. Assignment uses a recorded seed
($4301$), and the build is byte-deterministic --- archive timestamps are pinned
to a constant so the same inputs produce the same hashes. Because a component
never straddles a boundary, a system cannot see the \keep{} variant of a
scenario in training and the \supersede{} variant at test.

The gates that certify this are executable, not narrative, and two of them are
the reason v4.3 exists.

\paragraph{The shortcut audit.} Fitted on train, evaluated on hidden, and run
across five seeds, it trains word-level and character-level TF-IDF logistic
regressions over \emph{twelve restricted views} of each item ---
\path{full_text}, \path{dialogue_only},
\path{query_only},
\path{candidate_only}, \path{previous_turn}, \path{last_two},
\path{target_evidence_only}, \path{memory_only},
\path{user_only}, \path{assistant_only},
\path{first_three}, and \path{id_only} --- plus
punctuation-only and length-only numeric features and decision trees at depths
one through five. Each view has a pre-set accuracy ceiling (for example $0.40$
for \path{query_only} and \path{memory_only}, $0.45$ for
\path{candidate_only}, $0.65$ for \path{full_text}). The release ships
with \texttt{failed\_gates} empty: no probe cleared its threshold. The
\path{id_only} probe deserves particular note --- it confirms the opaque
identifiers themselves leak nothing --- as does \path{target_evidence_only},
which checks that even the correctly indexed evidence, stripped of its
dialogue context, does not linearly separate the classes.

\paragraph{The duplicate audit.} It hashes seven restricted views and fails on
\emph{any} cross-split collision in the five decision-relevant ones, then
computes character TF-IDF cosine similarity across every cross-split pair and
fails on any edge at $0.995$ or above. Because components share an evidence
pool by construction, within-split near-duplicates are expected and permitted;
the gate is specifically about leakage across the boundary.

Public model-facing records exclude gold labels, design targets, component
metadata, private identifiers, and annotator metadata. Hidden inputs, hidden
gold, final human gold, completed annotation packets, private mappings, and the
detailed restricted-view audits live only in a gitignored local boundary.
Content is addressed by two Merkle roots, one over the final gold
(\texttt{437b2a26\dots}) and one over the annotations
(\texttt{a7e59f88\dots}), and each distributed archive carries its own SHA-256.

\subsection{Human validation, and the weight it can bear}

Two separately completed human-annotation packets cover all $900$ items. The builder does not
accept them on trust: each row must carry a schema version, a content hash that
matches the coordinator's binding for that item, an action in the permitted set,
a non-empty list of evidence turn identifiers that all reference real turns in
that dialogue, a rationale of at least $20$ characters, a confidence in
$[0,1]$, boolean ambiguity and exclusion flags, an annotator identifier, and a
timezone-aware timestamp. Coverage must be exactly $900$ with no duplicates.
Any violation raises and aborts the build. The requirement that annotators cite
turn identifiers is the substantive one: it makes an annotation checkable
against the item rather than a bare label.

The packets agree on every action --- raw agreement $1.0$, Cohen's $\kappa =
1.0$, zero exclusions, zero adjudications --- with mean confidence $0.96$ and
$0.967$. Both flagged all $300$ \abstain{} items as intentionally ambiguous
\emph{scenarios}, which records designed ambiguity rather than uncertainty about
the label.

We are deliberate about what this does and does not establish. Perfect
agreement on a task whose labelling rule is deterministic by construction
certifies that the manual is followable and that the items instantiate it; it
does \emph{not} independently validate the construct, because two annotators
applying the same unambiguous rule will agree whether or not the rule measures
memory governance. Completion is accepted through dataset-owner attestation:
the builder verifies records, coverage, hashes, and agreement, but does not
authenticate reviewer identity or annotation duration. That is a provenance
limitation, and we report it as one rather than as an independent audit.

\subsection{Reading the result the design makes possible}

Because the three actions within a component are surface-matched, an error is
diagnostic rather than merely wrong: predicting \supersede{} on the \keep{}
variant means the system read a sentence that was present but not indexed. We
tested this directly. Of the $28$ errors the frozen controller makes on the
development split, $18$ can be mechanically resolved against a sibling item's
indexed pair, and in \emph{all $18$} the evidence supporting the predicted
action was present in the item but indexed to a different slot. Every
resolvable error is distractor capture --- the precise failure the component
design isolates. The instrument is measuring what it was built to measure.

\subsection{The \mgpc{} evaluation contract and staged protocol}
\label{sec:v43contract}

For \mgpc{}, a system submits exactly one action per public identifier using two
fields, \texttt{public\_id} and \texttt{action}. The evaluator rejects missing,
duplicate, unknown, and extra identifiers, so partial coverage cannot be
silently averaged away --- an incomplete submission fails rather than scoring.
It reports accuracy, macro-F1, per-class precision/recall/F1, the full confusion
matrix, coverage, selective coverage and accuracy, and component-cluster
bootstrap intervals.

Evaluation is staged to make the hidden split usable exactly once.
Train is reserved for engineering checks. Development carries all prompt,
configuration, guard, ablation, cost, and latency selection. Before any hidden
prediction, the provider and exact model identifier, prompt and system-prompt
hashes, decoding parameters, seed, retry policy, timeout, concurrency,
evaluator version, adapter version, split and dataset hashes, code commit, and
environment lock are frozen into a signed configuration record. Hidden inputs
are then predicted once per pre-registered configuration; the prediction file is
coverage-validated and hashed \emph{before} the local private scorer is allowed
to open hidden gold.

At the time of writing this procedure has been completed exactly once, for the
selected public-indexed-evidence ASK live controller using
\texttt{qwen/qwen3.5-9b} through OpenRouter at temperature $0$, seed $4301$,
$128$ maximum new tokens, and concurrency $1$. Its $540/540$ predictions were
coverage-validated and sealed with SHA-256
\texttt{e6a5987991\dots f846806} before scoring, with the raw-response and
manifest files hashed alongside. One transport-level remote disconnect occurred
after $432$ durable predictions; the runner's immutable-manifest check admitted
only the same frozen controls, and the resumed invocation produced the remaining
$108$ records with no duplicate identifier and no changed prediction. No hidden
record influenced any prompt, model, or configuration choice. The direct
consequence, which we restate wherever the hidden number appears, is that there
is \emph{no} hidden baseline, ablation, or paired comparison: only one
configuration was authorised, so every contrast in this paper against baselines
and ablations is a development-split contrast.

\subsection{The composed controller under test}
\label{sec:v43controller}

The system evaluated on \mgpc{} is not a bare prompt. It composes two
components that we can score separately on development data. A
\emph{public-evidence rule policy} reads only the fields a model may see and
emits one of five verdicts --- direct documented slot change, standing value
confirmed or proposal retracted, temporary or scoped exception, unresolved
scope/authority/timing, or no decisive public evidence --- each mapping to an
action. In parallel a \emph{live model policy} is prompted with the typed state,
the indexed evidence positions, and the three action definitions, and returns a
single JSON action. A conservative guard then composes them: it overrides a
model \abstain{} only when the rule policy has found decisive public evidence,
and never overrides a model commitment. The guard is deliberately asymmetric,
because converting an unnecessary abstention into a justified write is cheap
whereas converting a justified abstention into a write is the failure the
instrument exists to detect.

Two properties of this design matter for reading the results. First, the guard
is auditable per item: every prediction carries the model action, the rule
verdict, the rule that fired, the composed action, and the SHA-256 of the
evidence text it read. Second, because the composition is asymmetric, the
composed system can only move probability mass \emph{out} of \abstain{}, so any
increase in false commits is attributable to the guard and is directly
measurable (Section~\ref{sec:results:guard}).

\subsection{The \mgp{} pilot: composition and the leak it carried}

The remainder of this section documents the historical pilot. It is retained
because the v4.3 design decisions above are only legible against the failure
that motivated them, and because its per-item predictions remain in the release.

\subsubsection{Conflict taxonomy and distribution}

The pilot contains $180$ scenarios, $30$ for each of six scenario families,
with an action imbalance of
$90$ \supersede, $66$ \abstain, and $24$ \keep{} that we handle by reporting
macro-F1 and per-action results rather than accuracy alone.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llr l@{}}
\toprule
Type & Diagnostic condition & Rows & Gold action(s) \\
\midrule
T1 & preference drift over time & 30 & 30 \supersede \\
T2 & episodic factual correction & 30 & 30 \supersede \\
T3 & stable-preference replacement & 30 & 30 \supersede \\
T4 & ambiguity / referent uncertainty & 30 & 30 \abstain \\
T5 & transient capture trap & 30 & 24 \keep, 6 \abstain \\
T6 & confidence laundering & 30 & 30 \abstain \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
T1--T3 target supersession under different durability regimes; a subset of $10$
rows per type is \emph{cue-free} (\textsf{implicit\_conflict}), i.e.\ the
perturbation contains none of the explicit change cues (``now,'' ``instead of,''
``actually,'' \dots) yet still fixes a mutually exclusive current value. T4/T6
require \abstain{} because evidence is referentially uncertain or laundered
through an unreliable source; T5 separates a durable preference (\keep) from a
temporary override. Some supersession cases also have minimal-pair T4 partners.
These pairs test a narrow cue change, but they do not remove the global shortcut:
conflict type still determines the action throughout v2.

\subsubsection{Query modes}

Every scenario carries one query mode used only at the answer stage:
\emph{direct recall} ($108$ items), \emph{premise resistance} ($45$; the query
embeds the stale/unsupported value as a false presupposition that a correct
answer must reject), and \emph{implicit application} ($27$; a concrete task
solvable only by using the active value, with neither value named in the query).
These modes let us measure whether a system that makes the right write decision
also refuses stale premises and applies the active value in context.

\subsubsection{Splits and why family disjointness was not enough}

Splits are $60$ train / $30$ dev / $90$ test, stratified so each type contributes
$10/5/15$ rows and T5 contributes $8/2$, $4/1$, $12/3$ \keep/\abstain. No
template-family identifier and no minimal-pair group crosses a split boundary,
which blocks literal family overlap. It does \emph{not} block construct leakage
when the same family-to-action rule holds in every split. We therefore treat the
$90$-item test split as a held-out item split only, never as independent
validation.

\subsubsection{Pre-specified validity checks}

Before comparing policies we fit lookup baselines on train+dev and evaluate them
on test using only (i) the six-way type, (ii) the finer conflict type, and (iii)
source/reliability/temporal metadata. We also compute macro-accuracy and paired
bootstrap intervals by \texttt{template\_family\_id}; for
\texttt{minimal\_pair\_group\_id}, unpaired items are singleton clusters. A
shortcut baseline that approaches the full policy is a failed construct-validity
check, not a competitive result.

\subsubsection{Schema, invariants, and scoring}

Each row provides a dialogue prefix, prior memory state, perturbation event,
source/temporal metadata, a gold action, an auditable gold memory state, the
query mode, the expected answer, and a regex judge specification with required
(\texttt{include}) and forbidden (\texttt{not\_include}) spans. Evaluation is
two-stage: the write stage sees only the prefix, prior state, and perturbation
and commits an action and predicted state \emph{before} the query is revealed;
the answer stage then answers from the predicted state. A validator enforces the
state-transition invariants of Section~\ref{sec:actionspace} (one active record
per slot, retained superseded predecessors, single-successor lineage,
byte-equivalent \abstain{} states, active-only answers), and a released corpus
prints \texttt{VALIDATION PASSED: 180 examples, 0 errors}.

We score two families of metric (full definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}):
\emph{decision-level} --- action accuracy, per-action and macro-F1, and the
action false-commit rate on gold-\abstain{} items --- and \emph{memory-state}
--- state invalid-commit on gold-\abstain{} items, five-condition lineage
integrity on \supersede{} items, and stale-retrieval rate (the fraction of answers surfacing a forbidden
superseded value). Two shipped forgetting-rate metrics saturate at $1.0$ for every
system and are excluded from all tables as uninformative.

\subsubsection{Annotation: one independent annotator plus a model audit panel}
\label{sec:annotation}

We are precise about the annotation evidence, which has two separate components
and must never be described as multi-annotator human agreement.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0em,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
\item[One independent human annotator] re-labelled the $90$-item test split
against the annotation manual. Raw agreement with gold is $0.9889$
($\text{CI}_{95}\,[0.9667, 1.0]$), Cohen's $\kappa = 0.98146$
($\text{CI}_{95}\,[0.93997, 1.0]$), with exactly one disagreement
(\texttt{MGP6V2-T3-TEST-016}, an \textsf{implicit\_conflict} \supersede$\to$\abstain{}
case). This is $n{=}1$ annotator; the annotator's human status rests on author
attestation, not on evidence in the released files.
\item[A four-model audit panel] (four distinct LLMs) independently labelled the
same $90$ items: Fleiss $\kappa = 0.9815$ across the panel, with the majority
vote matching gold on all $90$ items ($88$ unanimous). This is a model audit, not
human inter-annotator agreement.
\end{description}
The larger human review sheet ($180$ rows) exists but is unfilled; a genuine
multi-annotator human release is future work (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
The panel and the single annotator may share the manual's reading because the
labelling policy is unambiguous by construction --- a construct-validity caveat we
carry explicitly.

\subsubsection{Construction grounding}

Scenario \emph{shapes} were informed by inspecting real corpora --- LongMemEval
knowledge-update items and its false-premise (\texttt{\_abs}) questions, Memora
\citep{uddin2026memora}
operation chains, GateMem's machine-readable judge structure, and named
confidence-laundering patterns --- but every released row is a newly authored
synthetic paraphrase; no source content is reused. This grounds the failure modes
without importing personal data, and it is the reason \mgp{} shares construct
names (implicit conflict, premise resistance) with concurrent read-side work
\citep{chao2026stale}, which we cite to disambiguate.
