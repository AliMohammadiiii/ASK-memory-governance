\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We separated memory writing from the state and answer layers it affects, then
used that separation in three ways: to expose a failed pilot, to construct a
better controlled diagnostic, and to inspect the deployed controller at
decision-level resolution.

The audit produced a negative result worth retaining. On \mgp{}, conflict type
alone predicts every held-out action without reading the dialogue. That failure
motivated \mgpc: $900$ items in $100$ surface-matched semantic components,
split by component and exactly action-balanced, with shortcut probes enforced
as release gates. A single pre-registered configuration run once on the sealed
$540$-item hidden split reaches $0.8481$ accuracy and $0.8479$ macro-F1. Its
confusion structure matters more than its rank: $30$ ambiguous-item errors
preserve the state and lose an audit record, while $8$ replace an active record
on insufficient evidence. The latter rate is $0.0444$, not the aggregate
$0.2111$ false-commit label.

The five-dimensional framework is now explicit and empirically traced. Every
candidate is evaluated by extraction confidence, evidence quality,
negation/hedge burden, signed candidate--prior relation, and a type stability
prior, followed by type-conditional thresholds and a discrete negative-relation
guard. Exact replay recovers all $12{,}654$ V12 decisions. Marker burden carries
the abstention branch, relation carries supersession, and the guard prevents
$11{,}091$ score-only replacements. Evidence quality and the stability prior
move margins but no action on this run; common type thresholds change only five.
This is both a contribution and a correction to the intended story: the policy
is transparent, but its effective runtime dimensionality is smaller than five
on the observed support.

Downstream, the homogeneous \lme{} run places governed writes at $76.1\%$,
above every structural baseline and $12.8$ points above naive overwrite, with
the largest gain on knowledge update. Trace accounting adds a second systems
finding: extraction and relation classification consume $92.8\%$ of prompt
tokens, while the answer stage conventionally scored by memory benchmarks is
only $7.2\%$. The contribution is therefore not merely an action vocabulary or
an accuracy number. It is a reproducible chain from evidence features, through
an auditable write and lineage transition, to a separately measured answer.

The bounds remain important. The scorecard has no per-write gold on \lme{} and
scores at chance through the \mgpc{} development adapter; only one v4.3 hidden
configuration was authorised; the diagnostics are synthetic, English-only,
single-slot, and single-step; the V12 result is one slice with one answer model
and judge; and monetary cost is unavailable. A credible successor needs
independent construction, natural multi-step trajectories, blinded multi-human
labels, and pre-authorised hidden baselines and ablations. Within those limits,
the paper's novelty is the controlled conjunction and its audit trail:
three-way write governance, typed evidence scoring, lineage-preserving state,
construct-validity gates, exact decision replay, and layer-separated
evaluation --- not priority for any individual mechanism.
