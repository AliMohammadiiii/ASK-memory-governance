\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ASK treats a persistent-memory write as a governed commitment. A controller may
preserve the compared record, replace it while retaining history, or refuse the
proposed commitment when evidence is insufficient. The controller is separated
from the component that mutates storage, and the resulting action, state,
lineage, retrieval, and answer can be inspected independently. This contract is
the paper's contribution. It is not reducible to an action vocabulary,
scorecard, database, or diagnostic dataset.

The repository supports this claim at complementary levels. The reference core
provides executable transition and invariant semantics. \mgpc{} makes the
write decision directly observable under controlled evidence. One configuration
frozen before hidden scoring reaches $0.8481$ accuracy and $0.8479$ macro-F1 on
$540$ items. Its confusion matrix separates $30$ state-preserving
\abstain$\to$\keep{} errors from $8$ state-changing
\abstain$\to$\supersede{} errors. The historical v2 result also matters:
conflict type alone recovers every held-out action, so v2 cannot support a
capability claim.

The \lme{} V12 final candidate tests ASK inside a longer pipeline. Governed
writes produce $137/180$ correct answers, compared with $114/180$ for naive
overwrite under the same slice and model protocol. Its $12{,}654$ logged
decisions replay exactly, and the mechanism audit shows that marker burden,
signed relation, and a discrete relation guard determine nearly all observed
actions. LongMemEval does not supply gold actions for those writes, so this is
downstream and execution evidence rather than direct controller accuracy.

The implementation boundary is equally important. The evaluated SQLite runtime
uses transactional supersession, retained predecessor identifiers, active-only
retrieval, historical traversal, and decision logs, but it does not invoke the
generic ASK invariant validator or emit the generic transition event. V12 also
retains two provenance bookkeeping gaps, and MGP-Natural remains quarantined
without independent gold or publication clearance. ASK therefore offers a
defensible framework for auditable write governance and failure attribution,
not a certificate of safe or general long-term memory. MGP-6, LongMemEval, and
the executable tests are evidence about that framework, not its identity.
