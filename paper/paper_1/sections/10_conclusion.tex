\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We separated memory writing from the state and answer layers it affects, then
used that separation to audit an author-designed pilot rather than merely report
its highest score. The central empirical result is negative and useful:
conflict type alone perfectly predicts the pilot's held-out actions, while the
transparent reference policy and structured LLM are statistically unresolved
and exchange safety for responsiveness under different costs. The release makes
these limitations reproducible through per-item predictions, shortcut probes,
cluster-aware uncertainty, finite-sample risk bounds, and lineage metrics.
Complementing the diagnostic, a homogeneous full-slice \lme{} run provides the
first eligible downstream result on this track: the governed controller reaches
$76.1\%$ and ranks above every structural baseline, evidence of downstream
benefit relative to those baselines even though the pilot cannot adjudicate the
reference-versus-LLM comparison. A credible successor must be independently
constructed, contain all actions within each conflict family, use blinded
multi-human labels, and test multi-step natural trajectories. Until then, \mgp{} is a pilot for evaluating the evaluator, not
evidence of general memory-governance performance.
