\section{2.1 Memory in language-agent
systems}\label{memory-in-language-agent-systems}

\emph{Memory} covers several mechanisms with different mutability and
deletion semantics: context-window memory, retrieval memory, summary
memory, structured records, and model parameters. Long context does not
remove governance obligations --- long-context models use evidence
unevenly by position \citep{liu2024lost} --- and retrieval-augmented
generation separates storage from generation without providing a write
policy \citep{lewis2020retrieval, borgeaud2022improving}.

The systems line most relevant to ASK treats memory as an agent-managed
store. MemGPT frames the LLM as an operating system that self-edits
tiered memory \citep{packer2023memgpt}; its open-source successor Letta
is evaluated in Chapter 7 (0.678 action accuracy under the favourable
NOOP→ABSTAIN mapping). MemGPT's memory edits are opaque function calls:
there are no write-time decision criteria, no abstention, no lineage.
Mem0 productionizes an extract--consolidate--retrieve pipeline whose LLM
chooses ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP per item \citep{chhikara2025mem0}; its
NOOP means ``nothing new'', not ``too ambiguous to commit'', and it
exposes no supersession lineage (Chapter 7: 0.622 upper bound,
invalid-commit rate 1.0). Zep/Graphiti stores a bi-temporal knowledge
graph in which contradicted edges receive invalidation timestamps
instead of deletion \citep{rasmussen2025zep} --- the strongest shipped
precedent for supersession-with-history --- but invalidation is a side
effect of LLM extraction, not a governed decision with an abstain
option, and Zep never scores whether an invalidation was correct. MemOS
proposes MemCube versioning/provenance and uses the word ``governance''
\citep{li2025memos}, but publishes no decision rule for
supersede-vs-keep-vs-refuse; through its adapter, supersession is not
even observable (Chapter 7: bound-only). A-MEM evolves
Zettelkasten-style notes but always integrates new information
\citep{xu2025amem}; MemoryBank forgets on an Ebbinghaus schedule ---
time-driven, not evidence-driven \citep{zhong2024memorybank}; Generative
Agents accumulate an append-only stream with reflection
\citep{park2023generative}.

Two recent lines are closer to ASK's decision framing. Memory-R1 trains
an RL memory manager over a discrete ADD/UPDATE/DELETE/NOOP action space
\citep{yan2025memoryr1} --- the clearest prior formalization of writes
as actions --- but the policy is opaque (PPO/GRPO), the reward is
answer-level, and NOOP is not uncertainty-driven abstention. RMM
performs prospective merge-or-add at session end with retrospective
RL-refined retrieval \citep{tan2025rmm}; its merge-vs-add echoes
supersede-vs-keep but uses similarity thresholds without contradiction
semantics or abstention. MemGuard assigns each memory an explicit
functional role (type) at write time to prevent cross-type contamination
\citep{ha2026memguard}; ASK's no-typing ablation (macro-F1
0.9399→0.5680, KEEP F1→0.0) makes the same argument experimentally.

\section{2.2 Memory lifecycle, revision, and
conflict}\label{memory-lifecycle-revision-and-conflict}

FadeMem classifies new-vs-old memory pairs (compatible / contradictory /
subsumes / subsumed) and applies decay or fusion \citep{fademem2026}; it
always acts on a detected conflict --- there is no abstain --- and
suppresses rather than preserving lineage. PAMU updates preference
signals only when drift is significant, via sliding-window and EMA
fusion \citep{sun2025pamu}: a conservative, interpretable update
trigger, but for preference vectors only, with no discrete action space.
TOKI is the closest conceptual neighbor found in the verified audit
\citep{wang2026toki}: it argues that contradiction resolution \emph{is}
write-time concurrency control, types four production heuristics as
bitemporal operators, and preserves the losing fact in an audit row. Its
await-confirmation operator is a formal ancestor of ABSTAIN and its
audit row parallels supersession lineage. Reddy and Challaram's
deterministic conflict-resolution result \citep{reddy2026freshness}
motivates testing interpretable update logic on independent data; it
does not validate the author-designed MGP-6 pilot by analogy.

\section{2.3 Benchmarks and evaluation for agent
memory}\label{benchmarks-and-evaluation-for-agent-memory}

LongMemEval evaluates chat assistants on five long-term memory abilities
including knowledge updates and abstention \citep{wu2025longmemeval}; it
is this thesis's downstream substrate (a stratified 180-item slice), and
it is end-to-end only --- it cannot attribute failure to the write
decision. LoCoMo tests very long conversational memory at scale
\citep{maharana2024locomo}. HaluMem separately scores extraction,
updating, and QA and reports that hallucinations accumulate at
extraction/update \citep{halumem2025}; it detects hallucination rather
than scoring a governed action choice, and it does not use gold
KEEP/SUPERSEDE/ABSTAIN actions or lineage and premise-resistance
metrics. The contribution here is therefore the operationalization and
controlled, layered evaluation of this particular policy combination,
not a claim of primacy for operation-level evaluation or decision
typologies.

STALE is a high-priority benchmark neighbor \citep{chao2026stale}: 400
expert-validated implicit-conflict scenarios probing State Resolution,
Premise Resistance, and Implicit Policy Adaptation. It shares MGP-6's
constructs almost by name (MGP-6's query modes include
\texttt{premise\_resistance}; its single annotation disagreement is an
\texttt{implicit\_conflict} item). The decisive difference is scope:
STALE probes a frozen agent's \emph{read-side} recognition that its
memory is stale, whereas MGP-6 scores \emph{write-time actions} with an
abstain option, supersession lineage, and memory-state metrics.
Supersede isolates the ``supersession gap'' on the LongMemEval
knowledge-update subset and trains it with GRPO
\citep{patel2026supersede}; its reward is answer-time fact currency
rather than a write-action taxonomy with abstention, lineage, or an
interpretable policy. GateMem uses the phrase ``memory governance'' for
a different axis --- access control, deletion compliance, and utility
across principals in shared memory pools \citep{ren2026gatemem}. RSCB-MC
applies risk-sensitive contextual bandits with explicit abstention to
memory \emph{retrieval} in coding agents \citep{iscan2026remember}.
Together these verified neighbors preclude any primacy claim for
abstention, governance, update control, provenance, or operation-level
measurement.

Benchmark and dataset documentation follows the spirit of datasheets and
model cards \citep{gebru2021datasheets, mitchell2019modelcards}; the
agreement statistics of Chapter 5 use Cohen's κ
\citep{cohen1960coefficient}, and the interval estimates of Chapters
5--7 use bootstrap methods \citep{efron1993bootstrap}.

\section{2.4 Belief revision and knowledge
editing}\label{belief-revision-and-knowledge-editing}

The AGM theory of belief change --- expansion, revision, contraction
under minimal-change postulates --- is the formal ancestor of
KEEP/SUPERSEDE \citep{alchourron1985theory}; classical AGM has no native
abstain (non-prioritized revision covers declining input). ASK is best
read as an operational, resource-bounded approximation of
non-prioritized revision for LLM memory; no formal AGM compliance is
claimed or proven. Parametric knowledge editing (ROME, MEMIT) evaluates
edits in weight space with efficacy/generalization/specificity
\citep{meng2022rome, meng2023memit} --- the decision-level-evaluation
analogue for parameters; editing assumes the edit should happen, whereas
ASK governs \emph{whether} to commit at all.

\section{2.5 Selective prediction and
abstention}\label{selective-prediction-and-abstention}

Abstention descends from the reject option \citep{chow1970reject},
selective classification
\citep{elyaniv2010selective, geifman2017selective}, and learning to
defer \citep{madras2018predict, mozannar2020consistent}. ASK applies
that established idea at memory-write time. TOKI's await-confirmation
and RSCB-MC's retrieval-time abstention further narrow the contribution.
The defensible result is a within-generator mechanism signature plus
explicit false-commit/missed-update trade-off, not a general safety
effect.

\section{2.6 Personalization and preference
modeling}\label{personalization-and-preference-modeling}

PrefEval shows preference following collapses within about ten turns
zero-shot \citep{zhao2025prefeval} --- external motivation for the
single-session-preference failure line in Chapter 8, with the difference
that PrefEval measures following while this thesis attributes failure
mechanism (grounding supply vs answer-side discipline). PersonaMem
benchmarks dynamic user profiling across sessions with frontier models
around 50\% \citep{jiang2025personamem}; LaMP is the canonical
retrieval-augmented personalization suite with static profiles
\citep{salemi2023lamp}.

\section{2.7 Provenance and
auditability}\label{provenance-and-auditability}

Provenance \emph{storage} is well-precedented: Zep's bi-temporal
validity intervals and episodes \citep{rasmussen2025zep}, TOKI's audit
rows \citep{wang2026toki}, MemOS's MemCube metadata \citep{li2025memos},
and HaluMem's evaluation-side error tracing \citep{halumem2025}.
Auditability as a governance requirement also appears in alignment and
risk frameworks
\citep{ouyang2022training, bai2022constitutional, nist2023airmf}. ASK
scores provenance and lineage ablations inside its pilot; the no-lineage
result demonstrates that action accuracy can miss lineage degradation,
but author alignment prevents a general causal claim.

\section{2.8 Positioning and allowed novelty
claims}\label{positioning-and-allowed-novelty-claims}

Following \texttt{reports/NOVELTY\_CLAIMS\_ALLOWED.md} and the verified
audit, the thesis makes one narrow contribution claim: it
\textbf{operationalizes and evaluates the combination} of an
interpretable KEEP/SUPERSEDE/ABSTAIN write policy, typed and
provenance-tracked records, lineage-preserving transitions, and separate
decision-, stored-state/lineage-, and downstream-answer measurements.
The controlled ablations attribute distinct failure signatures to
components of that combination. The thesis does not claim invention or
primacy for write operations as actions, update/versioning, provenance,
lineage, abstention, write gating, operation-level evaluation, or the
term ``memory governance''.
