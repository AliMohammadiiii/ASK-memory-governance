# Prospective-supervisor call agenda

**Purpose:** Test intellectual fit, identify a viable scope, and agree next steps.  
**Suggested length:** 25-30 minutes.  
**Status:** Planning document only; it does not imply that a call or supervision agreement exists.

## 1. Opening and objective - 2 minutes

- Thank Professor Vlachos for the time.
- State the goal plainly: assess whether the research problem fits his current programme and whether it could become a competitive Cambridge PhD proposal.
- Do not ask for an immediate supervision commitment at the start.

## 2. Research problem and thesis - 4 minutes

One-sentence problem: current fact-checking systems usually produce one answer from one evidence snapshot, while real evidence can be insufficient, ambiguous, corrected, duplicated, withdrawn, or temporally superseded.

One-sentence thesis: trustworthy verification should separately decide when to commit, maintain a revisable provenance-rich evidence state, and ensure later communication remains faithful to the active state.

Clarify the novelty boundary: the proposed contribution is a longitudinal task and controlled evaluation connecting selective commitment, state revision, and state-faithful communication. It is not a claim to invent abstention, iterative retrieval, provenance, belief revision, or temporal verification.

## 3. Three research questions - 4 minutes

1. Which observable signals support calibrated search, deferral, commitment, and revision under risk, coverage, and cost constraints?
2. Which state representations remain stable under irrelevant or duplicated evidence but responsive to decisive corrections and contradictions?
3. When do state-derived generation constraints reduce unsupported, stale, or false-premise-following outputs without making answers unhelpful?

## 4. Fit and differentiation - 4 minutes

- Explain the connection to AVeriTeC and Ev2R for real-world evidence and evidence evaluation.
- Ask how closely the direction overlaps with current work on ambiguity, temporal evidence, incremental evidence, or forecast updating.
- Explain the Master's boundary: personalised persistent memory supplied the evaluation discipline; the PhD would study public evidence, source dependencies, temporal validity, open-world acquisition, and fact-checking verdicts.

## 5. Questions for Professor Vlachos - 8 minutes

- Is the same-claim longitudinal framing scientifically useful, or is another unit of analysis more compelling?
- Which closest projects or unpublished work create the greatest overlap risk?
- Is 300-500 trajectories with three to five snapshots a realistic core, or should the first-year pilot be narrower?
- Would authentic trajectories plus controlled interventions be acceptable, and which existing resources are most suitable?
- Which first-year result would be publishable even if the full state architecture does not outperform simpler baselines?
- Should selective commitment and state revision remain one thesis, or should one become the centre and the other a supporting study?
- Are professional fact-checker partnerships feasible, or should the core remain independent of such access?
- Which methodological training should be front-loaded: fact-checking annotation, calibration, temporal reasoning, or retrieval evaluation?

## 6. Practical supervision and application questions - 4 minutes

- Is Professor Vlachos considering new PhD students for the intended entry cycle?
- Would he prefer a revised two-page summary, full proposal, code/repository sample, or transcript next?
- If the project becomes a good fit, what changes would he need before considering permission to be named in the application?
- Are there other Cambridge academics who should be disclosed or involved, without implying joint supervision?

## 7. Close and next steps - 2 minutes

- Repeat the two or three changes agreed during the call.
- Confirm who will send what and by when.
- Ask permission before naming Professor Vlachos in the formal application; do not infer permission from general encouragement.
