# Cambridge PhD application requirements - verified notes

**Course:** PhD in Computer Science, University of Cambridge
**Verification date:** 13 July 2026
**Target cycle:** 2027-28, whose exact course deadlines were not yet published on the current course-directory page at the verification date.

These notes distinguish verified current requirements from cycle-specific details that still need confirmation.

## Research proposal

Verified: the Department asks for a proposed-research statement of **no more than 3,000 words**. It must show knowledge of existing work, identify an area for new work, state concrete first-year goals and deliverables, and indicate how the applicant will achieve them. The statement is distinct from a personal statement. The Department invites applicants to contact an intended supervisor before formal application. Source: [Research Proposal - Department of Computer Science and Technology](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/admissions/phd/research-proposal).

The Department's public page does not specify whether references are excluded from the count, so this package reports a conservative count including references.

## Prospective supervisor contact and permission

Verified: the Department recommends an informal approach with a CV, transcript, and tailored covering email or statement showing research interests, alignment, relevant work, and motivation. It suggests a gentle follow-up after two weeks if there is no response. Source: [Before applying for a PhD](https://www.cst.cam.ac.uk/postgraduate-admissions/applying-phd).

Verified: the course-directory page says an applicant should contact a prospective supervisor and discuss the proposed research. Before naming someone on the application, the applicant should indicate that permission has been received, the research can be undertaken in the Department, and the supervisor can accept new PhD students. Source: [PhD in Computer Science - How to Apply](https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/directory/cscspdpcs/apply).

Package rule: all documents use **prospective supervisor** and make no claim of agreement. Formal naming remains blocked until explicit permission is received.

## Current application components

The course-directory page currently lists:

- two referees;
- transcript;
- CV/resume;
- evidence of competence in English, if required;
- mandatory research-proposal upload;
- one mandatory sample of work;
- a mandatory Reasons for Applying response of 1,500 characters;
- a mandatory Multiple Proposed Supervisors response of 200 characters; and
- a School of Technology widening-participation/contextual questionnaire.

The application is complete only after the form and supporting documents are submitted, any applicable fee is paid, and the referees provide their references. Source: [PhD in Computer Science - How to Apply](https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/directory/cscspdpcs/apply).

Portal fields can change between cycles. Recheck them after the 2027-28 application opens.

## Academic standard and English language

Verified: the course expects a first-class honours degree or equivalent; a Master's-level degree or other postgraduate work is highly desirable. International applicants should check country equivalence. Source: [PhD in Computer Science - Requirements](https://www.postgraduate.study.cam.ac.uk/courses/directory/cscspdpcs/requirements).

The same page currently lists these English thresholds:

- IELTS Academic: 7.5 overall and 7.0 in each element;
- TOEFL iBT: 107 overall and 25 in each element;
- C1 Advanced: Grade A or B, overall 193, no element below 185, plus a Language Centre assessment; or
- C2 Proficiency: Grade A, B, or C, at least 200, with no element below 185.

A CV statement such as "English B2" is a self-description, not evidence that these course-specific requirements have been met. Whether evidence is required and which evidence is accepted must be checked in the Applicant Portal and official guidance.

The requirements page also says that ATAS is required for the visa process after an offer and advises applicants not to apply for ATAS before receiving an offer.
