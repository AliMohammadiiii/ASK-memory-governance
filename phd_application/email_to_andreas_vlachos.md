# Draft email to Professor Andreas Vlachos

**Status:** Draft only - not sent.
**Suggested subject:** Prospective PhD research on revisable fact verification (2027 entry)

Dear Professor Vlachos,

I am preparing an application to the PhD in Computer Science at Cambridge for 2027 entry, and I am writing to ask whether a project on selective and revisable claim verification might fit your current research programme and supervision capacity.

My proposed research treats verification as a sequential commitment problem rather than a single retrieve-and-classify step. The system would decide when evidence is sufficient to search further, defer, commit, or revise; maintain a provenance- and time-aware evidence state across successive snapshots; and constrain later explanations so that they use active evidence and expose unresolved uncertainty. I would evaluate these capabilities separately at the commitment, stored-state, and communication layers. The intended contribution is a controlled longitudinal formulation and evaluation of their combination, not a claim to invent abstention, evidence updating, provenance, or temporal fact-checking individually.

The direction is motivated by your work on real-world evidence and evidence evaluation, including AVeriTeC and Ev2R, together with work on ambiguity, time-series verification, and incremental evidence in AmbiFC, Dis2Dis, TSVer, and PledgeTracker. It also appears compatible with the automated fact-checking topics listed on your [projects page](https://andreasvlachos.github.io/projects/), although I would expect the scope and framing to change in response to your advice.

My Master's research at the University of Tehran studies governance for personalised persistent LLM memory. It has given me experience in controlled task design, abstaining decisions, provenance and revision lineage, layered evaluation, and reproducible experiments. The proposed PhD is scientifically distinct: it concerns public evidence, source dependence, open-world acquisition, temporal validity, and fact-checking verdicts rather than personalised memory records.

I have attached my CV and a concise research summary. I would be grateful to know whether this direction is close enough to your current interests to merit a short conversation. If so, I can also send the full proposal and transcript in whichever format you prefer. I understand that this message does not imply supervision availability or permission to name you in an application.

Thank you for considering the enquiry.
